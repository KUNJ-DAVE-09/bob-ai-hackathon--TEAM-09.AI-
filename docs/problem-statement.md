# Problem Statement

## Who Experiences This Problem?

Logistics and supply chain operations teams at mid-to-large enterprises — freight forwarders, 3PL providers, manufacturers with complex global supply chains, and pharmaceutical cold-chain operators.

---

## The Problem

### 1. Disruption Cascades Are Invisible Until It's Too Late

Supply chain disruptions — weather events, port strikes, geopolitical crises — cascade across hundreds of active shipments simultaneously. Operations managers have no single view of which shipments are at risk, how severe the impact is, or what to do next. By the time a disruption is manually identified and communicated, delays have already compounded.

### 2. Fleet Assets Sit Idle While Other Routes Are Overwhelmed

When a disruption hits, vehicles and containers are misallocated. Trucks sit idle in unaffected regions while congested routes have no capacity. There is no automated way to match idle assets to urgent needs based on proximity and capacity.

### 3. Cold-Chain Failures Are Discovered Too Late

Temperature-sensitive cargo — vaccines, biologics, perishable food — travels across multiple carrier legs, each with its own refrigeration system. A temperature excursion on any leg can spoil a $500K+ cargo, but breaches are only discovered at the final delivery scan. By then, the cargo is unsalvageable and regulatory liability has already accrued.

### 4. Recovery Planning Is Manual and Slow

When disruptions do occur, building a recovery plan requires pulling data from multiple disconnected systems — TMS, ERP, carrier portals — and manually drafting executive communications. This takes hours that the business doesn't have.

---

## Impact

| Impact Area | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Delayed shipments | SLA breaches, customer penalties |
| Idle fleet assets | Wasted capacity, revenue loss |
| Cold-chain excursions | Cargo loss, regulatory fines, patient harm |
| Slow recovery planning | Extended downtime, leadership blind spots |
